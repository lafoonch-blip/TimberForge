# Outreach and call prep

Companion to [`INTERVIEW_KIT.md`](INTERVIEW_KIT.md) (the questions) and
`OUTREACH_LIST.local.md` (who to contact — kept out of this repository, see [`DIRECTORY_COUNTS.md`](DIRECTORY_COUNTS.md)). This file is the part
in between: how to get someone on the call, and what to have in front of you
when they pick up.

**Target:** 15–20 completed interviews. **Gate:** three foresters naming a tract
and a date. See [`BUSINESS_CASE.md` §9](BUSINESS_CASE.md#9-the-decision-gate).

---

## 1. The outreach message

Consulting foresters are sold to constantly — by mills, by software vendors, by
landowner-lead services. The only thing that distinguishes this message is that
it is true: you are not selling, and you want to be told you are wrong.

Keep it under 150 words. Longer reads as marketing.

### Email

> **Subject:** 30 minutes on how you run a cruise?
>
> Mr. ——,
>
> I found you in the [Virginia Department of Forestry directory / North Carolina
> Forest Service list] — you're listed for timber cruising in [county].
>
> I'm building cruise software and I'm trying to understand how the work
> actually gets done before I build any more of it. I'm not selling anything;
> there's nothing to buy yet.
>
> I'd like 30 minutes to hear how you run a cruise start to finish — where the
> hours actually go, what breaks, what you use now. I expect to find out I've
> got parts of it wrong, which is the point.
>
> Any weekday morning that works for you. I'm in [your location] and happy to
> drive out if you'd rather talk in person.
>
> Shayne Lafoon
> [phone] · [email]

### Phone

Most of this list published a phone number, and many of these are solo
practices where the forester answers it himself. Calling converts better than
email and gets you a decision in ninety seconds either way.

> "Mr. ——, my name's Shayne Lafoon. I'm building software for timber cruising
> and I'm calling foresters to understand how the work really gets done before I
> build any more of it. I'm not selling anything. Could I get 30 minutes of your
> time sometime this month to hear how you run a cruise?"

Then stop talking. If he asks what the software does, give one sentence — *"it
handles the prep and the compilation around the field work"* — and go straight
back to asking for the 30 minutes. Do not demo on the recruiting call.

### Rules

- **Call mornings.** Field work often starts early and runs through the middle
  of the day; late afternoon and evening catch people at a desk but tired.
- **Offer no compensation.** Willingness to spend 30 unpaid minutes is itself a
  demand signal, and paying destroys it.
- **Name the directory you found them in.** It shows you did not buy a list.
- **One follow-up, then stop.** A week later, two sentences. No third contact —
  this is a small, highly networked community and persistence reads badly.
- **Warm first.** Before any of this, work your existing LandForge users. A
  warm introduction converts several times better, and some of them may cruise
  themselves or hire cruisers.

## 2. Sequencing the list

`OUTREACH_LIST.local.md` has 62 named entries — 40 VA, 22 NC, 39 with a solo signal.
That is more than you need, which means you can afford to spend the early
conversations learning how to run the conversation.

**Start with 5 practice calls** from the entries you care least about
geographically. You will run Q2 badly the first two times — the hour-by-hour
reconstruction takes real discipline to hold someone to, and the instinct to
accept "about a week" and move on is strong. Burn those calls on people who are
not your ideal launch customers.

**Then work the solo VA entries.** The Virginia DOF listings record a literal
"Timber Cruising & Appraisals" service checkbox, so cruising is directly
sourced rather than inferred; and Virginia is the stronger of the two launch
states for stumpage. Solo practitioners feel the prep-and-reporting hours
personally, because there is nobody to delegate them to.

**Hold the multi-person NC firms for last.** They buy differently — often
someone other than the cruiser decides — and they will teach you less about
whether the core thesis holds.

Book no more than three interviews a day. The scoring sheet has to be filled in
within an hour of each call while you still remember tone, and that is 20–30
minutes of real work per interview.

## 3. Call prep sheet

Print this, or keep it on a second screen. One page.

### Before you dial

- [ ] Their directory listing open — county, years of experience, services
      checked, associations
- [ ] Scoring sheet open and blank (from `INTERVIEW_KIT.md`)
- [ ] Q2 table drawn out, ten rows, ready to write in
- [ ] Demo closed. Not minimised — **closed**
- [ ] Recording consent decided: ask at the top, and accept no gracefully

### The shape of the call

| Minutes | Part | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Thanks, restate why, ask about recording | No product talk |
| 2–14 | **Q1–Q6, their world** | **Say nothing about TimberForge** |
| 14–24 | Q7–Q10, walk the workflow | Narrate, don't pitch |
| 24–32 | Q11–Q13, commitment | Ask for a tract and a date |
| 32–35 | Q14, value vs spend | Last, after the commitment |

### The four things that decide whether the call was worth making

1. **The Q2 hours, stage by stage.** Prep and reporting versus field and travel.
   This is the number the whole thesis rests on.
2. **Q8, one feature.** Force a single answer. Scatter across ten features means
   no wedge.
3. **Q13, a tract and a date.** Not enthusiasm. A named tract.
4. **Q9, what you got wrong.** Ask it plainly and let the silence sit.

### Things you will be tempted to do, and shouldn't

- **Answering Q2 for them.** "So maybe half a day on the compilation?" — now
  it's your number, not theirs. Ask, wait, and let it be awkward.
- **Defending the product at Q9 or Q10.** Write it down, say "that's useful,"
  ask what else.
- **Reading a hedge as a yes.** "I'd definitely take a look at it" is a no.
  Record it as a no.
- **Demoing during recruiting.** Once they've seen it, Part 1 is contaminated
  and the call is worth much less.
- **Talking price.** Q14 is arithmetic about their own hours, asked last, after
  the commitment. It is not a pricing survey and must not become one.

### Closing, whatever the outcome

> "This was really useful — thank you. Two things: is there anyone else you'd
> point me to? And can I come back to you in a couple of months and show you
> what changed because of this?"

The referral ask is how you get past a directory listing into the actual
network. The second question is a soft re-contact permission that costs nothing
and keeps the relationship open even with a clear no.

## 4. Capture

Fill the scoring sheet in within the hour. Then, once a week, update three
things:

| Number | Where it lives | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews completed | this file, §5 | Progress against 15–20 |
| Tracts named + dated | this file, §5 | **This is the gate** |
| Median prep+report hours vs field hours | `BUSINESS_CASE.md` | The thesis |

Keep the `verified` / `modeled` / `unknown` tags intact when you move a number
into `BUSINESS_CASE.md`. Interview-derived figures are `verified` only where you
have the forester's own number, not your reconstruction of it.

## Reading it early

Do not wait for twenty. After **six** completed interviews, look at the Q2
tables together. If the hours are already clustering in field time and travel
rather than prep and reporting, the thesis is aimed at the wrong part of the
job, and finding that out in week two instead of month twelve is the entire
reason for doing this first.
