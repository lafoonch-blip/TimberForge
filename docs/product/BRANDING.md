# TimberForge Marketing & Branding Guide

## 1. Brand Positioning

**TimberForge** is a professional timber intelligence platform built for foresters and timber cruisers.

Core product promise:

> **Assess. Cruise. Value. Score.**

TimberForge should feel:
- Professional
- Modern
- Technical
- Field-ready
- Trustworthy
- Data-driven
- Practical
- Forestry-native

TimberForge should not feel:
- Rustic
- Vintage
- Overly outdoorsy
- Consumer-focused
- Like a generic GIS tool
- Like a traditional logging company

---

## 2. Relationship to LandForge

TimberForge is initially a standalone business/product, but its brand should fit naturally within the broader **LandForge** family.

Long-term relationship:

**TimberForge**
- Specialized timber intelligence
- Timber cruising
- Timber valuation
- Timber score

**LandForge**
- Broader land intelligence
- Consumes TimberForge score and timber metrics later

Recommended relationship language:

> **A LandForge company**

or, when product integration is more mature:

> **Part of the LandForge intelligence platform**

The brands should share:
- Similar typography
- Similar geometric design language
- Similar earth-tone palette
- Similar icon construction
- Similar clean, modern UI style

They should remain visually distinct enough to stand alone.

## 7. Visual Identity Direction

### Overall Style

The TimberForge visual identity should be:

- Minimal
- Geometric
- Contemporary
- Strong at small sizes
- Suitable for mobile apps
- Easy to reproduce as a monochrome mark
- Clearly related to LandForge

Avoid:
- Detailed illustrated forests
- Multiple trees inside complicated scenes
- Mountain landscapes
- Traditional forestry clip art
- Axes, saw blades, or lumberjack imagery
- Heavy gradients
- Vintage badges

---

## 8. Logo Direction

### Recommended Concept

Use a **modern geometric tree / timber mark integrated with the LandForge-style hexagonal geometry**.

Preferred characteristics:
- One primary tree or abstract forest form
- Strong negative space
- Simplified geometry
- Hexagon or partial-hexagon structure
- Subtle reference to a tree ring, parcel boundary, or growth line
- Works as an app icon
- Works in one color

Potential concept directions:

### Concept A — Geometric Tree + Hexagon
A simplified pine/tree silhouette created from clean angular shapes within a hexagonal frame.

### Concept B — Tree Ring + Parcel
An abstract tree-ring pattern combined with a parcel/grid geometry.

### Concept C — TF Monogram + Tree
A modern **TF** monogram where the negative space subtly forms a tree.

### Concept D — Timber Layers
Three clean stacked/angular layers suggesting:
- forest canopy
- timber inventory
- data layers

**Preferred direction:** Concept A or C.

## 9. Color Palette

The current forest/earth palette is directionally strong.

### Primary

**Deep Forest**
- Approx. `#0F3328`
- Primary brand / headers / logo

**Evergreen**
- Approx. `#345B45`
- Secondary brand tone

### Supporting

**Moss**
- Approx. `#71885D`
- Supporting data / UI elements

**Timber Brown**
- Approx. `#8B6843`
- Timber / land accent

**Warm Sand**
- Approx. `#D9CFBD`
- Background / supporting surfaces

### Neutral

**Warm Off-White**
- Approx. `#F7F5EF`

**Charcoal**
- Approx. `#242927`

The palette should remain restrained. Green should dominate; brown should be an accent rather than the primary brand color.

---

## 10. Typography

Typography should feel modern and technical rather than rustic.

Recommended direction:

### Headlines / Brand
- Clean geometric sans serif
- Medium-to-bold weight
- Strong uppercase wordmark where appropriate

Examples of style:
- Inter
- Manrope
- Geist
- Neue Haas Grotesk-style typography
- Avenir-style typography

### Body
Use a highly legible modern sans serif.

Avoid:
- Western fonts
- Rustic serif fonts
- Decorative outdoor fonts

---

## 11. Wordmark

Preferred treatment:

**TIMBERFORGE**

- Single word
- Strong geometric sans serif
- TIMBER and FORGE may use subtle weight or tone differences
- Do not overuse gradients
- Should read clearly at small sizes

Optional relationship lockup:

**TIMBERFORGE**  
*A LandForge Company*

---

## 13. Photography

Photography should emphasize professional forestry rather than generic outdoor recreation.

Preferred imagery:
- Foresters actively cruising timber
- Diameter tape measurements
- Basal area prism / relascope
- Field tablets or phones
- Managed timber stands
- Pine plantations
- Mixed hardwood stands
- GIS maps in real forestry contexts
- Logging roads and access
- Professional timber operations

Avoid:
- Generic hikers
- Campfires
- Recreational forests
- Lumberjack stereotypes
- Stock imagery that feels unrelated to professional forestry

## 16. TimberForge Score Messaging

Preferred language:

> **One score. Every important timber metric behind it.**

Other options:
- **A clearer way to understand timber assets.**
- **The score is simple. The data behind it isn't.**
- **Professional timber intelligence, summarized.**
- **Know what the timber is worth—and why.**

Do not market the score as a replacement for a professional cruise or forester judgment.

---

## 17. Brand Voice

TimberForge voice should be:

- Direct
- Confident
- Professional
- Technical when necessary
- Clear
- Practical
- Evidence-driven

Use:
> “Current sampling error is 8.4%. Two additional plots are recommended to reach the target.”

Avoid:
> “Our revolutionary AI will transform forestry forever.”

The brand should earn trust through useful information rather than hype.
